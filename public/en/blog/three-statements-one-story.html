The Three Statements Tell One Story

2026-03-04 · 8 min · Beginner

Profit, the balance sheet, and cash are three cameras on the same business. A stock only makes sense when the three pictures agree.

One business, three views

Public companies do not publish a single “health score.” Under IAS 1 they present a set of statements. TASI-listed companies report under SOCPA standards aligned with IFRS. U.S. companies file with the SEC. The labels differ. The questions do not.

Read one statement alone and you will miss the trick. Profit can rise while cash falls. Assets can grow because debt grew. Cash can look strong because the company stopped investing. The story is the combination.

What each statement answers

StatementQuestionWatch for
Income statementDid the business earn a profit this period?Revenue quality, margins, one-time gains
Balance sheetWhat does it own, and what does it owe, at this date?Debt, receivables, equity that is being diluted
Cash flowDid cash actually move?Operating cash versus net income, capital spending

Net income is an accounting result. Free cash flow is what remains after the spending required to keep the assets working. When those two diverge for several years, believe the cash until you understand the gap.

A cross-check before you buy

Before a ratio or a fair-value figure, ask four plain questions:

  1. Are sales growing, and are operating margins stable or widening?
  2. Is the balance sheet funding that growth with debt the earnings can carry?
  3. Does operating cash track profit, or is profit sitting in receivables and inventory?
  4. Is capital spending maintaining the business, or is management starving it to show cash?
Rule of thumb

If you cannot explain the difference between profit and operating cash in one sentence, you do not yet know the stock well enough to buy it.

Where to read a real filing

For a Saudi listing, open the company on the Saudi Exchange and read the financial statements, not only the price page. For a U.S. listing, use SEC EDGAR. The tutorials take each statement apart: start with the income statement, then the balance sheet, then why cash is the stricter test.
