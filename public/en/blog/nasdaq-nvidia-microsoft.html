A tech pullback that still left the week higher

Friday the 13th closed out a volatile week with a tech pullback. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 229.82 points, or 0.9%, to 25,136.27, while the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.2% and the S&P 500 lost 0.4%. The dip came after a strong start to the week, so all three major indexes still finished the week higher. That was their first weekly gain in five weeks.

Two triggers: inflation and Nvidia

A hot yearly PPI

October's Producer Price Index rose less than expected month over month, but the year-over-year rise was the highest since November 2022. That kept the Federal Reserve's rate-hike outlook in focus. Higher rates put the most pressure on growth stocks, and growth stocks dominate the Nasdaq.

Chips resumed the selling

The second trigger was Nvidia. Selling in chipmakers resumed, and the S&P 500 technology sector shed 1.7%. It was the worst performer of the 11 major sectors.

Nvidia: a leadership crisis, not yet a demand crisis

Nvidia announced on November 12 that CEO Jensen Huang had resigned. The story began with reports of a personal relationship and grew into a national-security issue. The New York Times reported that US intelligence officials believed Alice Huang (no relation), a Chinese executive linked to Singapore-based data-center firm Megaspeed, had been passing information to Beijing. China has called the spying claim a smear, and it has not been proven. CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO.

The stock has been hit hard. Nvidia recorded its sixth straight weekly decline, its longest losing streak since October 2012, and lost more than $600 billion in market value this week. Its market cap closed below $4 trillion. Analysts are cutting their views too. HSBC downgraded the stock to Hold and cut its price target from $365 to $170. Truist also moved to Hold, and Morgan Stanley lowered its target.

The fallout spread across the AI trade. AMD and Broadcom had rallied earlier in the week as traders bet on customers moving away from Nvidia. On Friday they gave back part of those gains: AMD fell 8.8% and Broadcom 4.8%.

Microsoft: an operational problem the market shrugged off

Microsoft had a very different kind of bad week. A global outage hit Outlook and Teams, and the shares were down 3% in premarket trading. Once services began coming back, the stock erased its losses and closed 0.2% higher. It held up even on a day when the tech sector fell.

The difference in reaction is the main lesson of the week. A fixable operational problem is a short-term headline, and investors look past it. A governance and leadership crisis raises questions about strategy, customers and regulators, so the market demands a bigger discount until those questions are answered.

Nvidia — governance shock

  • CEO resigned; interim CEO in place.
  • Board investigation and national-security questions. The spying claim has not been proven.
  • Six straight weekly losses, and more than $600 billion lost this week.
  • Analyst downgrades, including HSBC's cut from $365 to $170.

Microsoft — operational hiccup

  • Global Outlook and Teams outage, now easing.
  • Leadership and strategy unchanged.
  • Recovered from about −3% premarket to close higher.
  • Up about 27% over six months as of late October.

The bigger picture: concentration risk

Beneath the index decline, the market was healthier than the headline number suggests. More than 60% of S&P 500 companies rose on Friday. A handful of mega-cap tech stocks moved the indexes. That cuts both ways: when those names recover, the indexes can recover quickly too.

How a recovery could happen

Nvidia's next test comes on Tuesday, November 17, when it reports earnings. The report will let investors judge the business separately from the leadership crisis. If demand for its AI chips is still strong and guidance holds, the gap between the business and the share price could start to close. The company also has depth below the CEO: analysts note that several senior technologists report to Huang, so chip development would likely continue.

Microsoft's case rests on steady execution. Friday's outage did not change the long-term story. Repeated outages, though, could hurt confidence in Azure, its cloud business, and that is worth watching.

Three scenarios for the weeks ahead

The probabilities below are editorial estimates, not forecasts.

Bull case · about 25%

Earnings reset the story

Nvidia beats and holds its guidance, the board names a credible permanent CEO, and the investigation finds no business wrongdoing. Chip stocks rebound sharply, and Microsoft gains as AI spending fears ease.

Base case · about 50%

Choppy stabilization

Solid earnings help Nvidia find a floor, but the stock trades in a range while the investigation and CEO search continue. Microsoft keeps outperforming as a lower-risk way to own AI.

Bear case · about 25%

The crisis deepens

The investigation widens to China-related export controls or customer relationships, or guidance disappoints. Hot inflation brings Fed hikes back into focus, and the whole tech sector reprices lower, including Microsoft.

Is this an opportunity?

For long-term investors, pullbacks driven by headlines rather than weaker businesses have often been entry points. These two stocks offer different risk profiles.

Nvidia: higher risk, possibly higher reward

The stock is now about 13% below its November 6 close, but the investigation and leadership questions are not resolved. Waiting for Tuesday's earnings before acting, or buying in small steps, limits the damage if bad news comes.

Microsoft: the steadier name

It held up well this week, which makes it a more defensive way to stay exposed to AI. The watch item is whether outages start to repeat and dent confidence in Azure.

Whatever you choose, buying gradually rather than all at once and keeping position sizes reasonable is the disciplined approach in a market this concentrated.
