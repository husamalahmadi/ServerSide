Why the market is falling

AI and chip stocks under pressure

Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November, and the stock hit a 52-week low on Monday ahead of tonight's results.

New chip export rules

On 16 November, the US Commerce Department required licences to ship advanced AI chips to data centres in five countries, adding pressure on the sector.

Rates and yields

Inflation data came in hotter than expected on Friday. Markets are pricing another Fed rate hike in December, and the 10-year Treasury yield is around 5.1%.

AI valuation doubts

Investors are questioning whether heavy AI spending will pay off, and the market's largest weights are AI names.

What it means

Three down days on their own are not a crash. What matters is where they are happening.

Nasdaq 100

Monday's close of 27,197 sat right on the 27,200 floor of its range since May. The green candle needed to confirm the end of the correction has not come yet, and a close below 27,200 would delay it.

Dow Jones

The index is moving back toward the 50,700 floor of the sideways range it has held since August. Its uptrend was never confirmed, and TruePrice.cash's Accumulation indicator was already negative.

S&P 500

The index is trading near its 200-day moving average, a level many long-term investors watch closely.

In short, the market is testing major support levels just as several big events arrive. This is a decision zone, not a confirmed breakdown yet.

The view from TruePrice.cash

Already invested? Hold and don't panic.

Selling into a support test, a day before major news, often means selling near the low. Review your positions, but avoid emotional decisions.

Out of the market? Wait.

Don't try to catch a falling knife. Let the market hold its support levels and print clear green candles, with accumulation positive, before adding new money.

Manage your risk.

If your portfolio is heavy in AI and chip stocks, check that your exposure matches your risk tolerance. Volatility is likely to stay high in the coming weeks.
