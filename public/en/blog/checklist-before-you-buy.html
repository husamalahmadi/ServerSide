A Checklist Before You Buy a Share

2026-09-02 · 8 min · Beginner–Intermediate

A screener can find candidates. It cannot decide for you. These six checks are the minimum between a ticker and an order.

In this article
  1. Can you explain the business?
  2. Do the statements agree?
  3. Is the price below a range, not a wish?
  4. How much of the portfolio is this?

Can you explain the business?

If you cannot say how the company gets paid, who the customer is, and what would make that customer leave, you are trading a symbol. Read the annual report's description of the business before the ratios. On the Saudi Exchange that narrative sits with the financials. The same is true of a U.S. 10-K.

A durable advantage — a brand, a cost position, a network, a regulated franchise — is what lets good numbers last. The moat tutorial is the longer version of that question.

Do the statements agree?

Use the three-statement check from the earlier note in this blog, then add two filters:

  • Debt the company can service from operating earnings, not from new borrowing.
  • No red flag you cannot explain: receivables racing ahead of sales, repeated “one-time” charges, or cash that never appears.
Core idea

A fair-value figure on TruePrice.Cash is an input to this checklist. It is not a substitute for reading why the number looks high or low.

Is the price below a range, not a wish?

Write down what you think the business is worth and why. A single target with no range hides how sensitive you are to one assumption. If the market price is not below the low end of a range you would still defend after a bad quarter, you do not have a margin of safety. You have a hope that the quote goes up.

The DCF tutorial shows why small changes in growth or the discount rate move the result. The screening tutorial shows how to narrow a market before you do this work by hand.

How much of the portfolio is this?

Even a sound idea can be the wrong size. Decide the position before the order, and decide what evidence would make you sell or add. That evidence belongs in a short written thesis: the reason you own it, the numbers that would prove you wrong, and the date you will reread it. The closing tutorial in the series is how to write that thesis.

Then stop. More articles will not replace the filing of the company you are about to own.
